What's Working in Your Market

Content patterns in your category

Across 300 posts from comparable accounts in this category, the dominant content pattern is consistent: descriptive captions written in a mixed tone that blends practical information with personality. This is not a category built on hard-sell copy or viral hooks. Accounts that have scaled in this space, including @devwindsor (4.5M followers) and @windsorone (27,754 followers), both lead with description and maintain that mixed tone across their top-performing content. The pattern holds whether the audience is local or broad.

Your account follows the same structural instincts — description-led, mixed tone — which means your content voice is already aligned with what this category rewards.

Where you diverge is in two measurable ways. First, your captions average 456 characters against a category average of 357. That 28% gap is not disqualifying, but it means your posts ask for more reading time than most accounts your audience also follows. Second, your emoji density sits at 0.6 per 100 characters, while the category average is 1.3. Emojis function as visual breaks in a caption, giving the eye a place to pause before continuing. At roughly half the category rate, your captions read as comparatively dense.

These two gaps compound each other: longer text with fewer visual breaks.

The single most actionable shift is to add one or two emojis per caption as structural punctuation — not decoration — placed at natural pauses between sentences or thoughts. You can do this starting with your next post without changing a word of your actual copy.
