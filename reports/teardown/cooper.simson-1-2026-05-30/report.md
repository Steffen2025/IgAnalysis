## 1. Success Thesis

Cooper Simson’s account performs by packaging AI as immediate, job-to-be-done transformations: one prompt, one tool, one concrete outcome, often framed as something you can do right now with Claude or ChatGPT. The highest-performing posts combine a strong novelty claim (“can now,” “just leaked,” “free skill”), a highly specific use case (video, design, slides, backyard, room redesign), and a keyword-comment CTA that turns curiosity into public engagement. The comment-to-like ratio is unusually high, which suggests the audience is not just passively consuming but actively asking for setup guides, prompts, and templates. The content is overwhelmingly reel-led and tightly repeated around a small set of AI systems and use cases, indicating the account wins through message consistency, not variety.

**Pillars**

| Pillar | Evidence | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Instant utility framing | Top captions repeatedly promise a direct outcome in one sentence, e.g. “Claude can now create and edit full videos” and “Build a full slide presentation in 90 seconds with one Claude prompt.” | Replication should lead with the result first, then the mechanism, so viewers instantly understand what they can do and why they should care. |
| Specific novelty hooks | The most common openers include “claude can now,” “you can now,” and “harvard just leaked,” while 37% of hooks use numbers, signaling a bias toward newsy, time-sensitive, quantified claims. | A copycat strategy should anchor each post to a fresh capability, leaked insight, or quantified promise to create urgency and clickability. |
| Comment keyword conversion loop | 92% of captions use comment-keyword CTAs, and posts with CTAs average 1,530 comments versus 1,370 without, indicating a measurable lift from asking for a coded reply. | The account is engineered to convert attention into public comment activity and likely into DM/community funnel steps later, so every post should include a simple keyword ask tied to the promise. |
| Narrow but repeated solution stack | Hashtags are concentrated in a stable core set—#ai, #claude, #content, #productivity—with recurring secondary tags like #chatgpt, #design, #vibecoding, and #automation. | Consistency around a small tool-and-outcome stack helps the audience instantly categorize the account and understand what it’s for, which is easier to scale than broad AI commentary. |
| Reel-first, high-frequency distribution | 85% of analyzed posts are reels, cadence is 8.9 posts per week, and the median gap is 0.9 days, showing a high-output distribution system. | The playbook depends on frequent testing of similar formats to keep hitting breakout angles and to let the algorithm and audience learn the expected content pattern. |

**What's replicable**

- Use a repeatable caption formula: outcome first, then “Comment ‘X’ for the guide/prompt/template,” with the keyword matching the use case.
- Build posts around specific AI actions people already want: redesign a room, create a video, build slides, generate design assets, organize a project.
- Keep the tool language stable and recognizable by centering on one or two platforms, especially when the audience already associates them with the transformation.
- Use novelty language tied to the platform’s latest capability or a strong external signal (“can now,” “just leaked,” “free skill”).
- Maintain a tight hashtag system with a few core tags and a few use-case tags, rather than broad, disconnected tag sets.
- Post frequently enough to test many angles within the same message universe, rather than waiting for a single perfect post.

**Hard to copy**

- The account’s existing trust and familiarity with “Claude” and “Cooper” likely lower friction on clicks and comments.
- The extreme outlier posts show that one or more format-topic combinations have already been validated by the audience, giving this account a learning advantage.
- The current audience may be conditioned to comment specific keywords immediately, which is a behavior that takes time to build.

**Hook formulas**

- “[Tool/Agent] can now…” (capability reveal hook) — Example: “Claude can now create and edit full videos with animations and sound design with this skill”
- “You can now…” (permission + outcome hook) — Example: “You can now turn any idea into a full content pipeline using AI agents”
- “This AI agent…” (identity + promise hook) — Example: “This AI agent turns messy notes into a client-ready proposal in minutes”
- “[Big authority] just leaked…” (credibility shock hook) — Example: “Harvard just leaked that every major company is hiring for a job that didn’t exist 12 months ago…”
- “Comment ‘KEYWORD’ …” bait embedded right after the first-line promise (mechanic hook) — Example: “Comment ‘VIDEO’ for the setup guide + my best prompts”
- “Top [N] free … skills…” (list value hook) — Example: “Top 5 free Claude skills that replace a 6-figure creative studio”

**CTA strategy**

The account engineers comments as the primary conversion action: nearly every post uses a “Comment ‘KEYWORD’ for the setup guide + prompt/template” mechanic (commentKeywordPct is 0.92). The CTA is placed immediately after the first-line promise, so viewers understand what they’ll get (setup guide, exact prompts, prompt template, or install links) in exchange for commenting. Links are minimally used (linkInBioPct is 0.03) and DMs are absent (dmPct).

**Voice & tone**

The writing is punchy, almost announcement-style, focused on concrete outcomes (“create and edit full videos…”, “redesign any room…”, “build a full slide presentation…”). It uses high-contrast, benefit-first phrasing and leans into “free” and “exact prompts/setup guide” to reduce friction and boost intent to comment.

**Caption structure templates**

- 1) One-line first-line promise (usually starts with a capability/authority opener like “Claude can now…” or “Harvard just leaked…”)—kept relatively long but tight to the payoff
- 2) Immediate value re-statement (often repeats the outcome or specifies the format/timeframe, e.g., “in 30 seconds”, “in 90 seconds”, “full… with animations and sound design”)
- 3) CTA mechanic on its own line: “Comment ‘KEYWORD’ for the setup guide + my best prompts” or similar (keyword changes by post)
- 4) Optional swipe/next-step line for carousels (e.g., “Swipe to see what it is”) before hashtags
- 5) Hashtag cluster at the end (commonly includes #ai #claude plus niche tags like #design/#content/#productivity)

**Replication tips**

- Start the caption with a deterministic capability announcement using this exact style: “[Agent/Tool] can now… + specific deliverable” (video/edit/design/slide/shopping list). Keep it to a single strong first line.
- Use the comment keyword CTA in the second block, always mapping the keyword to the promised asset: “Comment ‘DESIGN’ for the exact prompts + setup guide” (swap KEYWORD based on the post theme).
- Add a precisionifier (time, scope, or transformation) to increase believability: “in 30 seconds,” “from a single photo,” “full slide presentation… in 90 seconds.”
- Keep CTAs consistent and explicit: include what they get (setup guide, prompt template, install links, shopping list template) rather than a generic “DM me.”
- Match the category vocabulary you see repeated in keywords: DESIGN, SKILLS, UGC, STACK, AUDIT, IMAGE, CLAUDE, COOPER, COUNCIL, FRAMES, CODES, AGENCY, LEARN, GOAL, RUN—use 1–3 of these per post in the body/CTA context, then finish with a #ai #claude cluster.
